# 100 print magazines for wealthy and design-literate readers: how they look

Compiled 22 November 2026 as an art-direction reference for a luxury chauffeur company's printed pitch magazine.

**How to read this.** Each entry gives name, publisher or country, reader, visual signature and one device worth borrowing. Two markers show how far to trust it:

- **[S]** = at least one design fact in the entry comes from a cited source (designer interview, Fonts In Use, magCulture, It's Nice That, foundry or publisher page). The sourced facts come first.
- **[L]** = light entry. The title is real and established, but I found no reliable design write-up in this pass.
- Everything after the word **(inferred)** in an entry is my own description from general familiarity with the title, not from a source. Check it against a physical copy before relying on it.

Count: 100 titles, 63 marked [S], 37 marked [L]. Entries run about 45–80 words; the light ones are deliberately shorter.

**Dropped from the starting list because I could not verify them as print magazines:** The World of Yachts, a Rolls-Royce owners' magazine, and magazines from Brunello Cucinelli, Aman and Rosewood. **Status caveats:** Departures stopped printing in 2021 and relaunched as a digital brand (a later return to print is reported but I did not confirm it); The Happy Reader may have ended its run; typefaces change often, so dates are given where known.

## 1. Luxury lifestyle and wealth

**1. Robb Report** — Penske Media, USA. *For:* ultra-high-net-worth buyers of cars, jets, watches, property. [L] Print and site were refreshed together in June 2017 after PMC took a stake. (inferred) Tall serif-caps masthead across the top, one hero object or car full-bleed, three or four short cover lines. Inside: glossy coated stock, object photography on clean studio or dusk backgrounds, serif headlines with small tracked sans labels, black/white/gold palette. **Borrow:** the "Best of the Best" annual franchise as a ranking device.

**2. Departures** — American Express, USA. *For:* Platinum and Centurion cardholders. [S] The 2021 relaunch (executive creative director Rachel Gogel) built a new identity system whose lead colour, a purple, mixes the legacy Departures red with Amex blue; that relaunch was digital-first after print ceased in March 2021. (inferred) The print era: wide-set serif masthead, one calm travel image full-bleed, few cover lines, generous margins, warm sunlit photography. **Borrow:** a single ownable brand colour derived from the parent brand.

**3. HTSI (formerly How To Spend It)** — Financial Times, UK. *For:* FT Weekend's affluent readers. [L] Renamed HTSI in mid-2022 under editor Jo Ellison, with a new masthead intended to feel less ostentatious. (inferred) Oversized format, compact four-letter sans masthead, one fashion or interiors image full-bleed with a single cover line; inside, big pictures, wide margins, small serif text, salmon-free white stock unlike the paper it travels in. **Borrow:** the single, quiet cover line.

**4. Elite Traveler** — Elite Luxury Publishing / New Statesman Media Group, USA. *For:* private-jet travellers; distributed aboard private aircraft. [L] Quarterly in print per its 2023–24 media kits. (inferred) Spaced serif-caps masthead, aerial resort or yacht photograph full-bleed, numbered "Top 100" lists, glossy stock, saturated blue-sky colour, heavy advertising rhythm. **Borrow:** the numbered-suite list format.

**5. Centurion Magazine** — Journal International for American Express, Germany. *For:* Centurion (black card) members. [L] Design Observer covered its 2004 launch as an exclusive members-only title promising "value, not clutter". (inferred) Dark, restrained covers with a small centred wordmark, heavy matt stock, large still-life and architecture photography, long captions, black/cream palette. **Borrow:** a cover with no cover lines at all.

**6. Billionaire** — HighEnd Media, Singapore. *For:* UHNW readers; not sold on newsstands. [L] Quarterly; each issue is themed ("The Exchange Issue", "The Savoir Faire Issue"). (inferred) All-caps sans masthead, art or portrait cover, themed issue title as the only cover line, thick perfect-bound spine, philanthropy and art-led picture stories. **Borrow:** naming every issue.

**7. Upward Curve** — Upward Curve, UK. *For:* corporate and private-jet charter passengers. [L] Listed as a quarterly print and digital title for private-aviation travellers. (inferred) Conventional glossy luxury layout: jet or destination cover, serif headlines, three-column text, advertorial-style brand profiles. **Borrow:** little; useful as the baseline to exceed.

**8. Spear's** — Spear's / Progressive Media, UK. *For:* private clients, wealth managers, family offices. [L] (inferred) Illustrated or caricature covers rather than photographs, serif masthead, dense three-column pages with rules between columns, drop caps, indices and ranked adviser tables; a newspaper-supplement seriousness in cream and navy. **Borrow:** illustrated portraits of people instead of headshots.

**9. Sabato** — De Tijd / L'Echo, Belgium. *For:* readers of Belgium's business dailies; weekend luxury supplement. [S] Alex Hunting Studio's redesign was described as comprehensive and "led by simplicity and space" (The Brand Identity; logged on Fonts In Use). (inferred) Large masthead, single image, almost no cover lines; inside, wide outer margins, small captions pushed to the page edge, quiet serif/sans pairing. **Borrow:** treating a weekly supplement like an indie quarterly.

## 2. Society and country

**10. Town & Country** — Hearst, USA. *For:* American old money and society. [L] (inferred) Wide serif masthead with ampersand as the ornament, portrait cover, moderate cover lines; inside, classic serif text, society-page grids of small captioned pictures, jewel-tone palette, coated stock. **Borrow:** the captioned party grid, reworked as a client or event page.

**11. Tatler** — Condé Nast, UK. *For:* British upper class and those watching it. [L] (inferred) Bold condensed serif masthead often partly hidden behind the cover subject, saturated colour backgrounds, witty cover lines in mixed weights; inside, "Bystander" party pages with tiny numbered captions, punchy pull quotes. **Borrow:** masthead overlapped by the subject to give depth.

**12. Country Life** — Future, UK. *For:* landowners, country-house buyers. [L] (inferred) Traditional serif masthead on a full-bleed landscape or house, very few cover lines; a famously long front section of full-page property advertisements, then the "Frontispiece" portrait page; natural-light architectural photography, green and stone palette. **Borrow:** the fixed, named opening portrait page.

**13. The Field** — Future, UK. *For:* shooting, fishing and hunting readers. [L] Published since 1853. (inferred) Heritage serif masthead, wildlife or sporting-dog cover, earthy palette, conventional three-column feature pages with drop caps and engraved-style spot illustrations. **Borrow:** small engraved vignettes as section markers.

## 3. Fashion and culture

**14. Vogue** — Condé Nast, USA. *For:* fashion's mass-luxury audience. [S] The cover title has used a Didot-style logotype since the 1950s (reported as 1955). (inferred) Masthead spans the full width and sits behind the cover subject's head; four to seven cover lines in mixed sizes; full-bleed studio or location portrait; inside, high-contrast didone display with a plain sans, well of uninterrupted full-page pictures. **Borrow:** the ad-free feature "well".

**15. Harper's Bazaar** — Hearst, USA. *For:* fashion readers with an eye for art direction. [S] HTF Didot was commissioned from Jonathan Hoefler in 1991 for Fabien Baron's redesign under Liz Tilberis, deliberately recalling Alexey Brodovitch's 1930s–50s pages. (inferred) Enormous hairline display letters cropped, overlapped and stacked as image; lots of white; text in narrow columns. **Borrow:** one huge letter or numeral used as a graphic shape on an opener.

**16. W** — W Media, USA. *For:* fashion and art insiders. [S] Logo redrawn around 2010 as a leaner single-stroke W; Fonts In Use records Benton Modern as core text (2012) and Signal Compressed titling for the 2018 Art Issue under design director Cian Browne. (inferred) Historically oversized format, single giant letter as masthead, portfolio-length photo stories with minimal text. **Borrow:** a one-letter masthead.

**17. The Gentlewoman** — Fantastic Man B.V., UK/Netherlands. *For:* women who read as well as dress. [S] Logo set in Futura; created with Jop van Bennekom and Veronica Ditting; issue 30 (art director Merel van den Berg) paired HB Set with Sharp Type's Tarnac for titles and text. (inferred) Tightly cropped portrait inside a coloured border, name of sitter as the only cover line; centred headlines, large justified text, mixed gloss and uncoated sections. **Borrow:** flat-colour cover frame that changes each issue.

**18. Fantastic Man** — Fantastic Man B.V., Netherlands. *For:* men interested in style over fashion. [S] D&AD praised clear typography and distinctive paper stocks; magCulture notes strict typographic rules, Times Roman, centred and justified setting, Founders Grotesk added as headline sans in issue 22, and a logo that moves between stacked-centred and other positions. (inferred) Black-and-white portraits, ruled boxes, newspaper-like bylines. **Borrow:** rules and boxes around headlines, set in one plain serif.

**19. System** — Paris/London. *For:* fashion-industry insiders. [S] Art director Thomas Lenthal (ex-Numéro); founders reacquired the title in December 2024. (inferred) Text-led: very long interviews, cover often a portrait with a dense block of names; small serif text in wide columns, plenty of white, portfolios on separate paper. **Borrow:** conversation transcripts as the main feature form.

**20. Self Service** — Ezra Petronio, France. *For:* fashion creatives and collectors. [S] A biannual hardcover book: issue 64 is 518 pages at 24 x 31 cm. (inferred) Board cover with a full-bleed fashion image and small stamped logo, heavy stock, long uninterrupted photo portfolios, Polaroid-style portrait series, handwriting and tape as graphic texture. **Borrow:** hard case binding for a "magazine".

**21. Purple** — Olivier Zahm, France. *For:* art-fashion readers. [S] Founded 1992; Zahm insists paper, layout and typeface all matter; magCulture describes issue 28's "bespoke, raw, headline typography" and unusual cover binding. (inferred) Thick book format, multiple covers, snapshot-flash photography, sections that switch paper and type. **Borrow:** changing paper stock to signal a change of section.

**22. AnOther Magazine** — Dazed Media, UK. *For:* fashion and culture readers. [S] Fonts In Use records Aspect for headlines and Ano for text in earlier issues; a new logo and art direction by Laura Genninger / Studio 191 arrived S/S 2015; art director Sarah-Jayne Todd by 2024. (inferred) Thick biannual, several cover versions, mixed papers, cinematic portraiture. **Borrow:** inserted booklets on different stock.

**23. Document Journal** — New York, USA. *For:* art, fashion and culture readers. [L] Biannual, printed in book format; editor and creative director Nick Vogelson. (inferred) Large trim, multiple covers, plain small logo, long artist-to-artist conversations, full-page photographs with thin white borders. **Borrow:** pairing two notable people in conversation.

**24. L'Officiel** — L'Officiel Inc., France. *For:* fashion readers; founded 1921. [L] (inferred) Wide serif-caps masthead, studio portrait cover, moderate cover lines; inside, classic didone display, archive imagery reused as graphic material, glossy stock. **Borrow:** archive pictures run beside new ones.

**25. Numéro** — France. *For:* high-fashion readers. [L] (inferred) Each issue is numbered and the number is the visual hook; bold sans masthead, striking portrait, minimal cover text; hard-flash, saturated photography; large type on openers. **Borrow:** the issue number as cover graphic.

**26. M Le magazine du Monde** — Le Monde, France. *For:* weekend readers of Le Monde. [S] The blackletter "M" masthead was extended in 2019 with the hairline serif Marian Black (Commercial Type); Marian Black has been in the palette since at least 2016 with Caslon No. 471/540 and Gotham; D&AD-awarded. (inferred) Photograph overlapping or cut by the M, white borders, spare inside pages. **Borrow:** a gothic initial paired with a hairline serif.

**27. 032c** — Berlin, Germany. *For:* fashion, art and politics readers. [S] Art-directed by Mike Meiré, whose 2007 redesign was the subject of Design Observer's "How to be ugly". (inferred) Red cover band and spine, stretched and squashed headline type, dense dossier sections, deliberate awkwardness against luxury advertising. **Borrow:** one aggressive brand colour on spine and cover edge.

**28. Acne Paper** — Acne Studios, Sweden. *For:* fashion-and-art readers; brand-published. [S] Thomas Persson's simple grid and typography were carried into Luncheon (magCulture); the title closed in 2013 and was later revived (revival inferred). (inferred) Very large format, themed issues, classical serif, black-and-white portfolios, matt paper. **Borrow:** oversized trim as the luxury signal.

## 4. Interiors and architecture

**29. Architectural Digest** — Condé Nast, USA. *For:* affluent homeowners and designers. [L] Redesigned under editor Amy Astley around 2017; the "AD" logo is described as light, high-contrast serif capitals. (inferred) Wide masthead, one full-bleed room, three or four cover lines; inside, big daylight interiors, serif headlines, caption blocks with small sans, glossy stock. **Borrow:** the single room cover with the owner's name as the headline.

**30. The World of Interiors** — Condé Nast, UK. *For:* decorators, collectors, aesthetes. [L] Hamish Bowles became editor in 2021. (inferred) Bookish serif masthead, eccentric interior or object cover, few cover lines; inside, long picture sequences, small text, generous captions, painterly natural light, never styled to look new. **Borrow:** the unhurried eight-to-twelve-page picture story.

**31. Cabana** — Milan/London. *For:* collectors of maximalist interiors. [S] Founded 2014 by Martina Mondadori, Christoph Radl and Gianluca Reina; biannual; each issue has several covers wrapped in real fabric or wallpaper from one producer, dispatched at random. (inferred) Huge volume, small logo on a stuck-on label, uncoated paper, pattern on pattern, warm film-like photography. **Borrow:** a material cover (cloth, textured paper) with a tipped-on title label.

**32. Apartamento** — Barcelona, Spain. *For:* people who like lived-in homes. [S] Logo and headings in a Clearface variant (Fonts In Use thread identifies Clearface Serial / B&P "Clerface"); magCulture notes pace created by vivid background colours and different paper stocks; spine carries the issue number large. (inferred) Small trim, uncoated paper, snapshot photography, Q&A interviews. **Borrow:** small format and soft, warm serif to feel personal.

**33. Openhouse** — Barcelona, Spain. *For:* creative people who open private spaces to the public. [L] Biannual. (inferred) Calm cover image with small centred title, heavy uncoated stock, natural-light interiors in ochre and plaster tones, wide margins, short serif introductions. **Borrow:** muted earth palette throughout.

**34. Ark Journal** — Copenhagen, Denmark. *For:* Scandinavian design and architecture readers. [S] Biannual, founded by editor Mette Barfod; 235 x 315 mm, about 240 pages; several cover versions per volume, numbered in roman numerals. (inferred) Soft daylight, pale wood and stone palette, large pictures with small restrained sans text. **Borrow:** roman-numeral volume numbering.

**35. Elle Decor** — Hearst, USA. *For:* style-led homeowners. [L] (inferred) Bold sans masthead, bright full-bleed room cover, several cover lines; inside, colourful saturated interiors, modular front-of-book product pages, cut-out objects on white. **Borrow:** cut-out product grids, used sparingly.

**36. PIN-UP** — New York, USA. *For:* architects and design insiders ("Magazine for Architectural Entertainment"). [S] Design director Dylan Fracareta since 2006; issue 1 was set entirely in Arial, Courier and Times New Roman, and Arial remains the house rule. (inferred) Oversized headlines, coloured paper sections, flash portraits of architects, playful jumps in scale. **Borrow:** one default font used at extreme sizes.

**37. Domus** — Editoriale Domus, Italy. *For:* architects; founded 1928 by Gio Ponti. [S] A different architect guest-edits each year towards the 2028 centenary; graphic design by Mark Porter; one artist signs all covers for a year (Edward Burtynsky for 2024, with Norman Foster editing). (inferred) Large format, strong grid, drawings and plans reproduced big, bilingual text. **Borrow:** one image-maker for a whole year of covers.

**38. Casa Vogue** — Condé Nast Italia (insert to Vogue Italia) and Brazil edition, Italy/Brazil. *For:* fashion-minded interiors readers. [L] (inferred) Fashion-magazine treatment of rooms: staged, graphic, colour-blocked photography, big didone or sans display, sparse text. **Borrow:** shooting interiors like fashion stories.

**39. Wallpaper\*** — Future, UK. *For:* design, architecture and travel readers. [S] The September 2013 redesign (Sarah Douglas, Lee Belcher) introduced custom Commercial Type faces: serif Portrait for the features well and sans Darby for the back section, replacing Plakat/Graphik; a later custom font, Copan, was made by studio Julia. (inferred) Asterisk logo, limited-edition artist covers, cool colour grade. **Borrow:** subscriber-only artist cover.

**40. House & Garden** — Condé Nast, UK. *For:* British decorators and homeowners. [L] (inferred) Classic serif masthead, pretty room or garden cover, several cover lines; inside, daylight interiors, floral and chintz palette, tidy three-column text, shopping pages. **Borrow:** little structurally; a reference for warm English tone.

**41. Frederic** — Schumacher, USA. *For:* decorators and design enthusiasts; brand-published. [L] (inferred) Large format, pattern-rich interiors, elegant serif display, generous white borders, heavy matt stock. **Borrow:** proof that a brand-owned magazine can read as editorial.

## 5. Slow and independent design titles

**42. Kinfolk** — Copenhagen, Denmark. *For:* slow-living, design-aware readers. [S] Alex Hunting's 2016 redesign brought a larger format and serif type; the 2021 tenth-anniversary redesign added custom Schick Toikka faces (wedge-serif text, deck and display cuts plus a sans, with alternate capital ligatures for headlines), a new paper stock, larger body text and dropped the cover's white bars. (inferred) Quiet portrait cover, muted palette. **Borrow:** capital-ligature headlines.

**43. Cereal** — Bath, UK. *For:* travel and style minimalists. [S] Co-founder Rich Stapleton art-directs and shoots much of it; sources describe a limited palette, "lots of un-inked space" and a pared-back look; Studio Faculty's 2019–21 redesign added a numbering system for articles. (inferred) Tiny tracked-caps logo, one desaturated image, no cover lines; grey, sand and white. **Borrow:** article numbering in place of section names.

**44. Monocle** — Monocle, UK/Switzerland. *For:* internationally mobile professionals. [S] Set entirely in Plantin and Helvetica. (inferred) Black cover band with white logo, a grid of small pictures and short cover lines, thick book-like spine, small text in several columns, mixed coated and uncoated sections, spot illustrations, yellow and black accents. **Borrow:** a two-typeface rule, never broken.

**45. Konfekt** — Monocle, Switzerland/UK. *For:* readers of "sharp dressing, drinking, dining, travel and design". [S] Quarterly since December 2020; 230 x 300 mm, 180 pages, printed in Germany; creative director Richard Spencer Powell. (inferred) Elegant serif logo, a single fashion portrait, warmer and softer than Monocle, cream paper, film-like colour. **Borrow:** cream stock plus one warm accent colour.

**46. Drift** — New York, USA. *For:* coffee and city-culture readers. [S] One city per issue; described as thick, perfect-bound, heavily photographed, free of advertising, with a "trademark aesthetic of calm control". (inferred) Full-bleed street photograph with a small wordmark, matt paper, documentary colour. **Borrow:** one place per issue as the organising idea.

**47. Sirene** — Italy. *For:* people drawn to the sea. [S] Printed on recycled algae paper; 32 x 24 cm, about 100 pages; D&AD describes its graphics as "rarefied, essential but never cold"; art director Sergio Juan. (inferred) Blue-tinted pages, lots of white, small type. **Borrow:** a paper stock that tells the brand's story.

**48. The Plant** — Barcelona/London. *For:* plant lovers with design taste. [S] Issue 12's redesign used the typefaces Verdigris and Union, a masthead redrawn by Seb McLauchlan and a more "immersive scale" of imagery; design led by Carol Montpart. (inferred) One plant per issue, uncoated paper, illustration mixed with photography. **Borrow:** a single subject per issue.

**49. MacGuffin** — Amsterdam, Netherlands. *For:* design readers interested in ordinary objects. [S] Each issue takes one object (bed, sink, rope); designer Sandra Kassenaar sets it in Churchward Newstype by Joseph Churchward. (inferred) Compact thick format, object cut-outs, dense captions, many paper changes. **Borrow:** an unusual revival typeface as the whole identity.

**50. Holiday** — Atelier Franck Durand, France. *For:* fashion-literate travellers. [S] Revived in 2014 after a 37-year gap; large 275 x 345 mm format with minimalist page design; each issue is devoted to one place. (inferred) Original 1940s-style script masthead, full-bleed fashion-travel photograph, classical serif text, matt paper. **Borrow:** a revived heritage masthead used at full width.

**51. Luncheon** — London, UK. *For:* art, fashion and culture readers. [S] Launched at 270 x 380 mm (later about 240 x 340 mm) on off-white matt Fedrigoni stock; the cover is built as a menu listing the contents; simple grid inherited from Acne Paper; a headline can run across several pages. **Borrow:** the cover-as-menu contents list.

**52. Rakesprogress** — London, UK. *For:* gardeners with urban taste. [S] 210 x 270 mm; magCulture notes an indie look of "comfortable space and gentle typography"; creative director Victoria Gaiger. (inferred) Painterly flower photography on dark grounds, uncoated stock. **Borrow:** dark-background still lifes.

**53. Port** — London, UK. *For:* men who read; style and long-form. [S] Matt Willey's launch design used his Port 1 stencil face for masthead and headlines, MFred for features and Quadraat for text; the 2023 redesign by Uncommon uses Triptych, Affairs, Serial D and a custom wide face, and each cover star handwrites the logo. **Borrow:** a hand-written title by the featured person.

**54. The Happy Reader** — Penguin with Fantastic Man, UK. *For:* book lovers. [L] (inferred) Slim, pocketable format, uncoated paper, coloured tinted pages, strict centred typography in the Fantastic Man manner, one long interview plus one classic book per issue. **Borrow:** a two-part structure the reader learns instantly.

**55. Noble Rot** — London, UK. *For:* wine and food enthusiasts. [L] (inferred) Illustrated covers by artists, bold hand-lettering, irreverent headlines, uncoated paper, flash photography of dinners. **Borrow:** commissioned illustration covers.

**56. Eye** — London, UK. *For:* graphic designers. [L] Sources here confirm its standing as the reference design quarterly. (inferred) Art-directed by Simon Esterson, typefaces change between issues, images reproduced large and precisely, multiple paper stocks, tightly edited captions. **Borrow:** caption discipline (every image credited and explained).

## 6. Travel

**57. Condé Nast Traveler / Traveller** — Condé Nast, USA/UK. *For:* affluent leisure travellers. [S] Custom faces by Type Supply: "Elegante" (debut 2017, design director Caleb Bennett) and the "Chill Gill" set; an earlier redesign used Leitura Display and Graphik. (inferred) Wide masthead, one place photograph full-bleed, two or three cover lines, sun-washed colour. **Borrow:** a soft, relaxed sans for labels.

**58. Travel + Leisure** — Dotdash Meredith, USA. *For:* upscale mainstream travellers. [S] Pentagram's 2012 redesign (Luke Hayman) used Superior Title and a custom Superior Text with PF Encore Sans, a logotype based on Sweet Sans, and an uninterrupted feature well; interiors changed again in 2015. **Borrow:** engraver-style sans capitals for the logotype.

**59. Suitcase** — London, UK. *For:* younger design-minded travellers. [L] Quarterly; two or three destinations in depth per issue. (inferred) Bold sans masthead, colour-saturated full-bleed cover, thick matt stock, film-grain travel photography, themed issue titles. **Borrow:** a one-word theme per issue.

**60. Yolo Journal** — New York, USA. *For:* well-travelled insiders. [S] Founded by ex-Condé Nast Traveler creative director Yolanda Edwards, who wanted the design to complement photography, not compete with it. (inferred) Large matt pages, full-bleed pictures with almost no type on them, small serif captions, contributors' personal snapshots. **Borrow:** pictures first, type out of the way.

**61. Lodestars Anthology** — UK. *For:* slow travellers. [S] One country per issue; photographic covers (photographers credited issue by issue); designer Thomas Harrison since issue 1; interiors mix photo essays with illustration. (inferred) Small centred title, uncoated paper, gentle colour. **Borrow:** hand-drawn maps.

**62. Afar** — Afar Media, USA. *For:* experiential travellers. [L] (inferred) Lower-case sans masthead, people-focused documentary cover, bright natural colour, clean modern grid, illustrated maps and front-of-book infographics. **Borrow:** people, not places, on the cover.

## 7. Watches, cars, yachts, aviation

**63. Revolution** — Revolution Media, Singapore. *For:* mechanical-watch collectors. [L] Founded 2005 by Wei Koh. (inferred) Large macro watch cover on black, bold masthead, glossy stock, tight movement close-ups, dense spec panels. **Borrow:** extreme macro detail photography of an object.

**64. Hodinkee Magazine** — Hodinkee, USA. *For:* watch enthusiasts. [S] About 8 x 10.75 in, 160 pages, lay-flat binding on semi-gloss stock, "softcover coffee-table book" feel, minimalist like the website; Fonts In Use contributors identify Söhne, Söhne Mono and Teodor in Volume 12. (inferred) One-image cover with a small logo. **Borrow:** lay-flat binding.

**65. The Rake** — Revolution Media, Singapore/UK. *For:* classic-menswear devotees. [L] Founded 2008; "the modern voice of classic elegance". (inferred) Serif masthead, tailored celebrity or archive portrait, warm rich colour, traditional serif typography, drop caps. **Borrow:** archive portraits of well-dressed icons.

**66. Magneto** — Hothouse Media, UK. *For:* collector-car owners. [S] Quarterly; D&AD-recognised design by art director Peter Allen; founded by the Octane co-founders to make "a magazine of the highest quality". (inferred) Heavy perfect-bound issue, single car on a clean ground, wide-set sans masthead, big photography with white space, illustrated openers. **Borrow:** one car, isolated, with nothing else on the cover.

**67. Octane** — Hothouse Publishing (now Hothouse Media), UK. *For:* classic and performance car enthusiasts. [L] Co-founded 2003. (inferred) Bold sans masthead, dramatic car photograph, many cover lines, glossy stock, classic newsstand grid with driving-shot spreads. **Borrow:** panning motion shots across a full spread.

**68. 000 Magazine** — SPY INK, USA. *For:* serious Porsche collectors. [S] Quarterly, more than 200 pages an issue, about $250 a year, shipped in protective mailers or a custom box; art director Justin Page. (inferred) Book-weight matt stock, very large photographs, archival documents, few advertisements. **Borrow:** delivery packaging as part of the product.

**69. The Road Rat** — UK. *For:* car culture readers. [S] 290 x 230 mm, 228–244 pages, over 1 kg, a mix of coated and uncoated heavyweight stocks, "understated design"; art direction Sam Walton and Jasper van den Bosch; cars shot in real settings; one limited cover was wrapped in quilted Nomex. **Borrow:** shooting vehicles in ordinary real locations.

**70. Type 7** — Porsche with Ted Gushue / Delius Klasing, Germany. *For:* Porsche and design-culture readers. [S] Published as numbered volumes: Volume Two is 21 x 27 cm, 412 pages, in a slipcase; Volume One had over 450 photographs. (inferred) Photo-book pacing, little text, cars beside architecture and art. **Borrow:** the slipcase.

**71. Boat International** — Boat International Media, UK. *For:* superyacht owners. [L] Relaunched with a new design in September 2014; senior art director Lee Franklin. (inferred) Wide tracked sans masthead "BOAT", aerial yacht on deep blue, few cover lines, heavy glossy stock, drone photography, interior spreads. **Borrow:** top-down aerial photography.

**72. Christophorus** — Porsche, Germany. *For:* Porsche customers; published since 1952. [S] Porsche's 2016 redesign aimed for "clear text and image language, a generous presentation and more air to breathe"; the 400th issue appeared in 2021. (inferred) Porsche-style sans, single car image, restrained cover text, large white margins. **Borrow:** issue numbering as heritage.

**73. The Official Ferrari Magazine** — Ferrari, Italy. *For:* Ferrari owners. [S] Three issues a year plus a Yearbook; Yearbook covers have been purely typographic commissions (David Pearson; Pentagram's Domenic Lippa for 2025). (inferred) Red and black palette, heavy stock, studio car photography. **Borrow:** a typographic annual cover by a named designer.

**74. Bentley Magazine** — Bentley Motors, UK. *For:* Bentley owners and prospects. [L] Launched 2003; quarterly; relaunched in 2018 by Archant Dialogue to modernise the brand's image; printed in English, German and Chinese. (inferred) Winged-B led cover, craft close-ups, dark green and cream. **Borrow:** materials close-ups (leather, veneer).

**75. Ramp** — Red Indians Publishing, Germany. *For:* car-culture hedonists. [L] (inferred) Very thick book-like issue, loud typography that changes each issue, cinematic staged photography, pop-culture references, heavy stock. **Borrow:** film-still storytelling around a car.

**76. Curves** — Stefan Bogner / Delius Klasing, Germany. *For:* driving-road enthusiasts. [L] (inferred) Each issue is one mountain route; photographs of empty roads with no cars or people, large landscape spreads, minimal captions, route maps. **Borrow:** the empty-road landscape as hero image.

## 9. Business and long-form with award-winning design

**83. The New York Times Magazine** — USA. *For:* Sunday Times readers. [S] The 2015 redesign (Gail Bichler, Matt Willey) introduced a full suite of custom typefaces by Henrik Kubel of A2-Type and a logo subtly redrawn by Matthew Carter; special issues get one-off lettering. (inferred) Concept-driven covers, often type-only. **Borrow:** a cover made of one typographic idea.

**84. T: The New York Times Style Magazine** — USA. *For:* style and culture readers. [S] Patrick Li's 2013 design used Schnyder (Commercial Type); the 2018 redesign under Hanya Yanagihara replaced it with the dense Kippenberger and Fact, and freed the shaded "T" logo to move around the cover. **Borrow:** a logo that changes position each issue.

**85. Bloomberg Businessweek** — Bloomberg, USA. *For:* business readers. [S] Richard Turley's 2010 design used Christian Schwartz's Neue Haas Grotesk with Publico on a 12-column grid, treated "as a long information graphic"; Druk (Berton Hasebe) was drawn for it around 2014; redesigned again in 2017 under Rob Vargas. **Borrow:** heavy condensed sans headlines with data set as design.

**86. WSJ. Magazine** — Dow Jones, USA. *For:* affluent Journal readers. [L] Magnus Berger was named creative director (Adweek). (inferred) Compact serif "WSJ." logo top left, celebrity or fashion portrait full-bleed, few cover lines, crisp didone display, large clean photography. **Borrow:** the full stop in the logo as a brand mark.

**87. FT Weekend Magazine** — Financial Times, UK. *For:* FT weekend readers. [S] A 2017 in-house refresh led by consultant Mark Leeds brought in Kris Sowersby's Financier (drawn for the FT's 2014 redesign) alongside Klim's Metric. (inferred) Illustrated or photographic concept cover, calm text pages, small pink brand accents. **Borrow:** a sharp, bookish serif for every headline.

**88. The Atlantic** — USA. *For:* serious general readers. [S] The 2019 redesign (Peter Mendelsund, Oliver Munday) replaced the wordmark with a large "A" monogram and introduced Atlantic Condensed by Jeremy Mickel, based on type from the 1857 first issue; expanded in 2022. **Borrow:** a monogram in place of a full masthead.

**89. The New Yorker** — Condé Nast, USA. *For:* literary and affluent readers. [S] Irvin display type (redrawn 2013 by Ben Kiel under Wyatt Mitchell), Adobe Caslon text and Neutraface for hierarchy. (inferred) Illustrated cover with no cover lines, a coloured strap at left, three-column text, spot drawings, cartoons. **Borrow:** the illustrated, line-free cover.

**90. Zeit Magazin** — Zeit Verlag, Germany. *For:* Die Zeit readers. [S] Creative director Mirko Borsche; famous for double covers, where the cover and the first inside page form a pair; one 2015 cover is tagged "one typeface, one size" using Dia. **Borrow:** the two-page cover reveal.

**91. Süddeutsche Zeitung Magazin** — Germany. *For:* SZ readers. [S] Art director Birthe Steinbeck; multiple D&AD pencils for covers and whole issues; commissioned custom lettering (Andrea Trabucco-Campos); Bourbon St. used for playful covers in 2021. **Borrow:** bespoke lettering for a special issue.

**92. brand eins** — Germany. *For:* business thinkers. [S] Designed since 1999 by Mike Meiré; strictly Sabon (regular and italic) with occasional Futura; described by Design Observer as a model of taste, precision and understatement. (inferred) Big statement-headline covers, conceptual photography. **Borrow:** an entire magazine in one serif.

**93. MIT Technology Review** — USA. *For:* technology decision-makers. [S] Redesigned in 2018 with Pentagram's Michael Bierut, drawing on MIT's graphic history; Design Week reports fresh sans-serif typography (specific faces not confirmed here). (inferred) Themed issues, bold typographic covers, strong grid. **Borrow:** a rigid modernist grid with one accent colour per issue.

**94. Wired** — Condé Nast, USA/UK. *For:* technology and culture readers. [S] The US 2013 redesign surprised with the didone Ambroise; a later custom display family by Abbreviated Projects sits between WIRED Mono and Apercu; Wired UK commissioned section faces from A2-Type and Sawdust. **Borrow:** a monospaced face for labels and data.

**95. Fortune** — Fortune Media, USA. *For:* executives. [S] The 2020 redesign by creative director Peter Herbert with Josue Evilla came with upgraded paper stock. (inferred) Wide sans logo, illustrated or portrait concept cover, ranked-list design, charts. **Borrow:** heavier paper as the first step of a redesign.

**96. Esquire** — Hearst, USA. *For:* men's general interest. [S] Earlier custom all-caps face Granger with Mercury Text; from late 2021 Flecha for cover and headlines with Publico text, plus TWK Lausanne and Glanz; tags note big type, loose letterspacing and type running across pages. **Borrow:** a headline that runs across the gutter.

**97. GQ** — Condé Nast, USA. *For:* style-minded men. [S] Gotham was commissioned for GQ (Tobias Frere-Jones, 2000) and used from 2001 until replaced in 2024 by proprietary faces also by Frere-Jones. (inferred) Two-letter block logo, celebrity portrait, saturated colour. **Borrow:** commissioning a typeface the brand owns.

**98. Vanity Fair** — Condé Nast, USA. *For:* culture and power watchers. [S] VF Didot by Commercial Type (2013, later released as Le Jeune) with VF Sans, Solano, Flama and Futura. (inferred) Wide didone masthead, group-portrait gatefold covers, many cover lines, long text with drop caps. **Borrow:** the gatefold group portrait.

**99. New York** — Vox Media, USA. *For:* metropolitan readers. [L] (inferred) Swash-serif logo, bold concept or photo-illustration covers, dense service pages, slab and serif mix, the back-page "Approval Matrix" chart. **Borrow:** a recurring back-page chart.

**100. National Geographic** — USA. *For:* general readers; photography-led. [S] Redesigned in 2018 with Godfrey Dadich; Type Supply lists custom faces "Earle" and "Marden" (attribution to this redesign inferred). (inferred) Yellow border, single photograph, few cover lines, wide margins, long captions. **Borrow:** a coloured border as the entire brand.
