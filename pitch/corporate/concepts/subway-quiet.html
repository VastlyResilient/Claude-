Absolute TransportationI · A Letter
Chapter I

A letter to Subway

Somewhere tonight, one of your people is landing after a long day, a long way from home. They are tired. They are thinking about tomorrow's meeting, or about the family they have not seen since Monday. What happens in the next ten minutes will never appear in a report. They will remember it all the same.

We built this company for those ten minutes. For the chauffeur already standing at arrivals, who knows their name. For the bag lifted before they reach for it. For the quiet car, and the feeling that someone thought about them before they arrived.

You carry a responsibility most people never see: the safety and the dignity of everyone who travels in your name. We would like to carry it with you. We answer the telephone ourselves, at any hour. We follow every flight. And we treat each traveler as though your own name were in the back seat, because in every way that matters, it is.

We would be honoured to look after the people of Subway, and to begin, if you will allow it, with a single journey.

Manmohan Komal

Chief Executive Officer

Chapter IV

In our care

A journey with us, as the person in the back seat will remember it.

IBefore they land
Their flight is already being followed. If it is early, so are we. If it is late, nobody has to call.
IIAt arrivals
A chauffeur who knows their name, takes the bag, and asks nothing of them.
IIIIn the car
Quiet, cool and immaculate. Conversation only if they want it, and discretion about everything that is said.
IVWhen plans change
One call to a person who can decide. A new route, a later pickup, a second vehicle: handled first, then reported to you.
VAt the door
Delivered to the entrance, not the kerb across the street, with their bags in hand.
VIAfterwards
If anything was less than it should have been, you hear it from us before you hear it from them.

“People don't remember what you sold them. They remember how well you served them.”

Manmohan Komal
